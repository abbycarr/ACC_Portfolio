Massachusetts Education Machine Learning Project

This project explores how qualities of Massachusetts' school districts impact students' access to higher education with visualizing and analyzing the correlations between variables, and testing and tuning multiple machine learning regression models on the data.

Source

Data Science course

Timeline

Oct- Dec 2020

Deliverables

Jupyter Notebook

The setup

Our project explores access to higher education for students in Massachusetts public schools. Specifically, we want to better understand what qualities might make a school district have higher rates of students who go on to attend college or university.

The insights from this project can be useful for further research. If these decision makers understand the characteristics that most impact access to higher education, they can form regulations and distribute resources in a way most likely to create more equitable public education systems across the state.

Data was acquired from Mass DoE and combined and cleaned. When working with our data and creating the project, we selected hypothesis tests about data behavior to see correlations and better decide on the final features. Further illustrating data relationships, we looked at heatmaps and scatterplots. We built Ridge, Lasso, k-Nearest Neighbor, SVM, and Multiple Linear Regression models. With our top 3 models, we fine-tuned our parameters to find the best performance. For Ridge regression we tested different alphas, or complexities, of the model and for k-nearest neighbors we could test different distance metrics and the number of n_neighbors.

Results

Although we continued with Multiple Linear, Ridge, and k-Nearest Neighbors models, the algorithm we recommend to be used for our predictive model is the Ridge Algorithm. In the end, hyperparameter tuning ended up most positively affecting the k-Nearest Neighbor algorithm but couldn't overcome the noticable overfitting to the training data. Our Ridge regression had a minor improvement to the composition of the training and testing scores, meaning that hypertuning our Ridge model is for the goal of avoiding overfitting to the training data.

Reflections

As a beginning Data Science project, this covers the fundamentals for working with models. I appreciate this project because, as we progressed, it showed clear growth in our understanding of the mechanics and uses of machine learning algorithms. Were we to go back, my group would be able to choose an even more useful question to ask in the scope of the available data.
